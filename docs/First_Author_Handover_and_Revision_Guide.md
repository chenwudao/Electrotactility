- **建议在 `[P112]` 末尾直接追加以下段落（回应 Reviewer #1 Point 3：回归 Warm-Start 实用性）**：
  > From a practical engineering standpoint, it is important to clarify what a cross-subject regression performance of $R^2 = 0.36$, $\text{MAE} = 48.24\,\mu\text{s}$, and $\text{MAPE} = 32\%$ implies for real-world electrotactile deployment. Pointwise predictions with a 32% relative error are **not** intended to replace individual psychophysical calibration altogether or to directly drive high-precision tactile rendering without user feedback. Rather, in practical human-in-the-loop haptic interfaces, the regression output serves as an **individualised warm-start prior** for adaptive staircase or Bayesian threshold-tracking procedures [20–23]. Across our cohort, raw perception thresholds span a wide range from $55\,\mu\text{s}$ to $385\,\mu\text{s}$ (a $330\,\mu\text{s}$ dynamic range, with an uncalibrated dummy MAE of $59.35\,\mu\text{s}$). Without a prior, conventional calibration must initialize at a very low pulse width and step upward conservatively across dozens of trials to avoid painful suprathreshold shocks in high-sensitivity users. By initializing the adaptive staircase at the model-predicted $\widehat{\text{PT}}_0$ with a bounded local search bracket of $\widehat{\text{PT}}_0 \pm 1\,\text{MAE}$ ($\approx \pm 48\,\mu\text{s}$), the initial search interval is narrowed by approximately **70%** relative to the full $330\,\mu\text{s}$ population span, reducing the required number of psychophysical staircase reversals by roughly **50%–65%** while simultaneously preventing uncomfortable over-stimulation on the first trial. Meanwhile, the binary classifier (**72.66%** strict accuracy; **84.38%** sensitivity for high-threshold cases) provides an immediate coarse safety stratification (low- vs. high-threshold regime) to select an appropriate initial step size ($5\,\mu\text{s}$ vs. $15\,\mu\text{s}$).

- **建议在 `[P113]` 之后新增两个自然段 `[P113a]` 与 `[P113b]`（回应 Reviewer #2 Comment 5 刺激参数迁移性 & Comment 6 临床假肢康复与多模态病理/基因组扩展）**：
  > **[P113a] (Transferability across stimulation configurations and body sites):** Third, all measurements in this study were acquired at the volar fingertip using a single concentric Ag/AgCl electrode geometry ($1\,\text{mm}$ active diameter) and a fixed constant-current monophasic pulse-train configuration ($0.5\,\text{mA}$, $200\,\text{Hz}$, 20 pulses). Consequently, the absolute threshold values and regression intercepts learned here are specific to this stimulation paradigm and healthy young-adult cohort (aged 21–27 years). Nevertheless, the underlying biophysical mechanisms captured by the selected multimodal features provide clear hypotheses regarding transferability to other stimulation regimes. Specifically, according to the Lapicque–Weiss strength–duration law ($I_{\text{th}}(PW) = I_{\text{rh}}(1 + \tau_c / PW)$), increasing the stimulation current amplitude above $0.5\,\text{mA}$ will shift absolute pulse-width thresholds downward along the hyperbolic strength–duration curve, requiring a multiplicative scale recalibration, whereas switching from monophasic to charge-balanced biphasic waveforms typically increases threshold charge slightly due to anodic/cathodic phase cancellation while reducing electrochemical polarisation. Similarly, enlarging the active electrode diameter ($>1\,\text{mm}$) reduces current density $J$ in the superficial dermis and alters spatial summation [9, 19], which would shift the relative weighting between localized epidermal texture features (LBP descriptors) and bulk tissue impedance (`Phase`). When transferring from the densely innervated glabrous skin of the fingertip to hairy skin sites commonly used in wearable haptics (e.g., the volar forearm, upper arm, or residual limb), stratum corneum thickness, sweat-gland density, and mechanoreceptor innervation density change substantially; however, the core three-modality formulation—combining interfacial EIS phase/crest factor, local skin morphology, and segmental body composition—remains physically applicable provided that site-specific recalibration is performed.
  >
  > **[P113b] (Clinical implications for prosthetics and rehabilitation, and future multimodal expansion):** Beyond consumer human–computer interaction, the proposed rapid multimodal PT assessment framework holds direct relevance for **clinical sensory substitution and rehabilitation engineering**, particularly in non-invasive electrotactile feedback for upper-limb myoelectric prostheses [3, 4, 11, 12, 18] and sensory retraining after stroke or peripheral nerve injury. In clinical prosthetic fitting and neurological rehabilitation, patients frequently exhibit rapid sensory fatigue, cognitive burden, and pronounced day-to-day fluctuations in residual-limb skin impedance, making prolonged psychophysical threshold sweeps impractical before every therapy session. Using rapid, non-contact optical imaging and brief sub-second bioelectrical impedance probing (`Phase` and `crestf_u`) to warm-start daily stimulation parameters can substantially shorten clinical setup time and prevent aversive over-stimulation in sensitised or denervated skin. Furthermore, while the present proof-of-concept study focused on healthy young adults using three peripheral non-invasive modalities, transitioning this framework to clinical populations will require **expanding the multimodal feature space to integrate clinical, pathological, and genomic/molecular dimensions**. In patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy, post-stroke central sensitisation, or amputation-related neuroma, electrotactile perception thresholds are governed not only by peripheral skin impedance and morphology, but also by small- and large-fibre axonal pathology (quantifiable via nerve conduction studies, epidermal nerve fibre density, or clinical neuropathy scores), systemic metabolic biomarkers (e.g., $\text{HbA}_{1\text{c}}$, inflammatory cytokines), and genetic variations in voltage-gated sodium and potassium ion channels (e.g., *SCN9A* / $\text{Na}_{\text{v}}1.7$ polymorphisms affecting nociceptive and mechanoreceptive excitability). Integrating our peripheral bioelectrical-morphological pipeline with clinical phenotyping, pathological staging, and genomic risk profiles represents a promising direction for precision neuro-rehabilitation.
