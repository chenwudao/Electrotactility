## Response to Reviewer #2

### Reviewer #2 — Comment 1 (Eliminating Feature-Selection / Subset-Size Leakage and Implementing Nested Cross-Validation)
> *"1. Possible data leakage in feature selection and subset-size selection. The pipeline includes Spearman-based redundancy filtering, tree-based ranking to retain the top 50 features, and a model-based search for the optimal subset. It is not stated whether these steps were performed inside each cross-validation fold or once on the full dataset. If they were performed on the full dataset, the test folds influenced feature selection and the reported accuracies are optimistically biased. In addition, the headline accuracies appear to be the maxima of the curves in Fig. 13, meaning the subset size was chosen using the same cross-validated results that are reported. The authors should use nested cross-validation n report the resulting accuracies, ideally with fold-wise variance or confidence intervals."*

**Response:**  
We thank the reviewer for raising this critical methodological point. In our original scripts, preliminary redundancy screening and feature ranking were indeed performed on the full dataset prior to cross-validation, which introduced optimistic bias into the previously reported binary classification accuracies.

To completely resolve this issue, we re-engineered the entire binary classification pipeline (**Section 2.4 `[P66]`, Section 3.2 `[P88]`, Table 3, and Figures 11–15**) under a unified **Double-Loop Nested Cross-Validation (`Outer 5-Fold + Inner 3-Fold`)** architecture (`run_all_unified_revision_closure.py`) so that **100% of preprocessing, feature scaling, Multi-Block PLS-VIP ranking, collinearity deduplication, subset-size/quota selection, and hyperparameter tuning are executed strictly inside each cross-validation training fold**:
1. **Outer Loop (5-Fold Subject-Grouped Split, 25–26 Training / 6–7 Test Subjects):** In cross-subject evaluation, the 32 participants (128 records) are partitioned at the participant level into 5 outer folds (`GroupKFold(n_splits=5)`), ensuring that all 4 palmar sites of any test participant are strictly unseen during training and feature selection.
2. **Inner Loop (3-Fold Nested Split, 17 Training / 8 Validation Subjects) for Leak-Free Feature Subset Selection:** Inside each outer training fold, an inner 3-fold subject-grouped CV loop evaluates candidate cross-modal subset quotas and tree depths. Within each inner training split, features in the global physiological ($p_1=50$), bioelectrical ($p_2=74$), and local texture/morphological ($p_3=945$) blocks are standardised using inner-training statistics and scaled by $1/\sqrt{p_m}$ for block variance parity. A 2-component Multi-Block PLS model is fitted exclusively on the training data to compute Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) scores, followed by greedy collinearity deduplication ($|r| < 0.85$ computed strictly on the training fold). Once the inner 3-fold loop selects the optimal cross-modal feature subset ($k=11$, quota $(4,5,2)$ for cross-subject CV; $k=12$, quota $(5,5,2)$ for non-cross-subject CV), **that exact feature subset is extracted from the outer training fold and kept strictly locked across all downstream classifiers in the outer loop**, which are then trained on the outer training fold and evaluated once on the held-out outer test fold.
3. **Updated Leak-Free Nested CV Results with Fold-Wise Variance (**Table 3**):**
   - **Cross-Subject Double-Loop Nested CV (Random Forest, $k=11$):** Primary strict accuracy ($\text{margin} = 0\,\mu\text{s}$) = **72.66%** (93/128; fold mean ± SD = **72.86% ± 4.29%**, 95% CI: **[69.10%, 76.62%]**; Balanced Accuracy = **72.66%**; Macro F1 = **75.52%**; ROC-AUC = **0.6793 ± 0.0956**). At $\text{margin} = 15\,\mu\text{s}$, accuracy is **81.25%** (104/128; fold mean **81.31% ± 8.91%**).
   - **Non-Cross-Subject Double-Loop Nested CV (Random Forest, $k=12$):** Primary strict accuracy ($\text{margin} = 0\,\mu\text{s}$) = **74.22%** (95/128; fold mean ± SD = **74.22% ± 6.45%**, 95% CI: **[68.56%, 79.87%]**; Macro F1 = **74.16%**; ROC-AUC = **0.7524 ± 0.0493**). At $\text{margin} = 15\,\mu\text{s}$, accuracy is **84.38%** (108/128; fold mean **84.35% ± 4.35%**).
   - **Cross-Model Comparison on the Locked Inner-Selected Feature Subset (Figure 11):** Evaluated on the exact same inner-selected feature subset per fold, the top four machine-learning classifiers from distinct algorithmic families against the random baseline (`RandomForest`, `ExtraTrees`, `KNN`, `SVM`, and `Baseline`) achieve **74.22%, 70.31%, 69.53%, 67.19%, and 50.00%** (non-cross-subject) and **72.66%, 68.75%, 67.19%, 66.41%, and 50.00%** (cross-subject) at $\text{margin} = 0\,\mu\text{s}$ (**84.38%, 81.25%, 79.69%, 78.12%, and 61.72%** non-cross-subject and **81.25%, 79.69%, 78.91%, 78.12%, and 61.72%** cross-subject at $\text{margin} = 15\,\mu\text{s}$).

All figures (**Figures 9–15**) and text have been updated with these exact leak-free Nested CV results.

### Reviewer #2 — Comment 2 (Tolerance Margin Analysis, Sample Counts in 141–171 μs, Recomputed Baselines, and Margin-Free Primary Metric)
> *"2. The tolerance margin inflates the headline results and should not be the primary metric. Without the margin, accuracy is 76.68% vs 76.07%. The authors should (a) report how many samples fall within 141-171 μs, (b) define precisely how predictions in the band are scored, (c) recompute the random/majority baseline under the same margin, since it is no longer 50%, and (d) report margin-free accuracy as the primary result."*

**Response:**  
We have fully implemented all four parts `(a)–(d)` in **Abstract (`[P12]`)**, **Section 2.5 (`[P72]`)**, **Section 3.2 (`[P88]–[P91]`)**, and **Table 3**:
- **(a) Exact number of samples within the tolerance bands (`margin_band_sample_statistics.xlsx`):** Around the median boundary of $T = 156.0\,\mu\text{s}$, the numbers of records falling within $[156.0 - m,\, 156.0 + m]$ across the 128 records are:
  - $m = 0\,\mu\text{s}$: **0/128 (0.00%)** (note: 5 records have $\text{PT} = 156.0\,\mu\text{s}$ and are assigned to the low-threshold class $\text{PT} \le 156.0\,\mu\text{s}$ under hard classification)
  - $m = 5\,\mu\text{s}$ ($[151.0, 161.0]\,\mu\text{s}$): **10/128 (7.81%)** (9 low-PT, 1 high-PT)
  - $m = 10\,\mu\text{s}$ ($[146.0, 166.0]\,\mu\text{s}$): **20/128 (15.62%)** (15 low-PT, 5 high-PT)
  - $m = 15\,\mu\text{s}$ ($[141.0, 171.0]\,\mu\text{s}$): **30/128 (23.44%)** (**20 low-PT records** in $[141.0, 156.0]\,\mu\text{s}$ and **10 high-PT records** in $(156.0, 171.0]\,\mu\text{s}$).
- **(b) Precise mathematical scoring rule:** In **Section 2.5 (`[P72]`)**, we explicitly define that for a tolerance margin $m \ge 0$, a binary prediction $\hat{y}_i \in \{0, 1\}$ for record $i$ with continuous threshold $y_{\text{cont},i}$ and true label $y_i = \mathbb{I}(y_{\text{cont},i} > 156.0)$ is scored as:
  $$\text{Correct}_m(i) = \mathbb{I}\!\left(\hat{y}_i = y_i \;\lor\; |y_{\text{cont},i} - 156.0| \le m\right)$$
  Thus, any record whose true continuous PT lies within $[156.0 - m,\, 156.0 + m]$ is deemed within the psychophysical boundary ambiguity zone and is not counted as an error.
- **(c) Recomputed random and majority baselines under the tolerance margin:** Because $N_{\text{band}}(m)$ samples are automatically accommodated, a 50/50 random guesser achieves an expected accuracy of $p_{\text{band}}(m) + 0.5\times(1 - p_{\text{band}}(m))$, which equals **50.00%** at $m=0$, **53.91%** at $m=5\,\mu\text{s}$, **57.81%** at $m=10\,\mu\text{s}$, and **61.72% (79.0/128)** at $m=15\,\mu\text{s}$. Similarly, the empirical fold-wise majority-class dummy classifier achieves **43.75% (56/128)** at $m=0$ and **57.03% (73/128)** at $m=15\,\mu\text{s}$. Both recomputed baselines are explicitly reported in **Table 3** and plotted in **Figure 11**.
- **(d) Margin-free accuracy (`Margin = 0 μs`) reported as the primary result:** Throughout the revised Abstract, Results, Table 3, Figure 11, and Figure 15, **$\text{margin} = 0\,\mu\text{s}$ is presented as the primary headline metric** (**74.22%** non-cross-subject, **72.66%** cross-subject), while $\text{margin} = 15\,\mu\text{s}$ (**84.38%** non-cross-subject, **81.25%** cross-subject) is clearly designated as a secondary sensitivity metric.

**Response:**  
1. **Definition of a single record (`Section 2.5 [P71]`):** We have clarified in the text that each of the 32 participants was tested on **four specific fingertips**: dominant index finger (`finger = 0`), non-dominant middle finger (`finger = 1`), non-dominant little finger (`finger = 2`), and non-dominant index finger (`finger = 3`), yielding $32 \times 4 = 128$ records.
